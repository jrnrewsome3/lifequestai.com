What Is AI, Really?

AI might sound like science fiction, but it's already part of your daily life. Let's start at the very beginning — no tech experience needed.

The Simple Explanation

AI stands for Artificial Intelligence. That sounds complicated, but here's a simple way to think about it: AI is a computer program that has "read" enormous amounts of text — books, websites, articles, conversations — and learned to have a conversation based on all of that.

Think of it like a very well-read librarian who has skimmed millions of books. They can answer your questions and help you find information — but they can also get things wrong, and they don't actually know you personally.

💬

Plain English Version

AI is a computer program that answers questions and helps with tasks by drawing on a huge amount of text it was trained on. It's not a person, it's not magic, and it doesn't always get things right.

Where AI Already Shows Up in Your Life

You may have used AI without even knowing it. Here are some examples you might recognize:

📱

Siri & Alexa

Voice assistants on your phone or smart speaker use AI to understand what you're saying.

🎬

Netflix Recommendations

The "you might also like" suggestions are powered by AI that studies what you've watched.

📧

Email Spam Filters

AI helps identify and move junk mail out of your inbox automatically.

🔍

Google Search

AI helps figure out what you're actually looking for, even if you don't word it perfectly.

What AI Is Not

Movies and TV have given us a lot of wrong ideas about AI. Let's clear those up:

✗

AI is not a robot that wants to take over the world

AI tools like ChatGPT and Claude are text programs. They don't have goals, feelings, or plans. They respond to what you type.

✗

AI is not always right

This is very important: AI can and does make mistakes. It can "hallucinate" — meaning it confidently states something that is simply not true. Always double-check important information.

✗

AI is not reading your mind

It only knows what you type to it in that conversation. It doesn't know who you are unless you tell it.

✗

AI is not a replacement for a doctor, lawyer, or financial advisor

It can help you understand general information, but for serious personal decisions, always talk to a qualified professional.

How AI "Thinks"

Here's an analogy that might help: imagine you're playing a word game where you predict what word comes next. "The sky is ___." Most people would say "blue." AI works similarly — it has learned patterns from massive amounts of text and predicts what a helpful, accurate response looks like.

This is why AI sounds so fluent and confident — it's been trained to sound helpful. But sounding confident and being correct are two very different things.

⚠️

Important to Remember

AI sounds very confident even when it's wrong. Think of it like a friendly person who sometimes makes up facts — they're not lying on purpose, but they can lead you astray. Always verify health, legal, or financial information from a trusted source like your doctor or bank.

The Most Popular AI Tools Right Now

In the next lesson, we'll go deeper — but here's a quick overview of the names you'll hear most often:

🤖

ChatGPT

Made by OpenAI. One of the first AI chat tools available to the public. Available at chat.openai.com.

💡

Claude

Made by Anthropic. Known for being thoughtful and careful. Available at claude.ai.

🔷

Microsoft Copilot

Built into Microsoft Word, Outlook, and Windows. Helps with everyday tasks in programs you already use.

🔍

Perplexity

An AI search engine that gives you answers with sources. Good for research and fact-checking.

✅

Safety Takeaway for This Lesson

  • AI is a tool, like a calculator — it can help you, but it can also give wrong answers
  • Never share your Social Security number, passwords, or banking information with an AI
  • If an AI tool asks for payment information upfront to answer a simple question, that's a red flag
  • The major AI tools (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot) have free versions — be suspicious of tools that charge just to chat

Let's Review

Before moving to Lesson 2, make sure you can answer these questions. There are no wrong answers — this is just for your own understanding:

  1. In your own words, what is AI?
  2. Can you name one place you've seen AI in everyday life?
  3. What's one thing AI cannot do (or should not be trusted to do)?

Key Terms

  • 📘 AI — Artificial Intelligence. A computer program trained to respond like a knowledgeable assistant.
  • 📘 Hallucination — When AI confidently states something false. Always verify.
  • 📘 Chatbot — An AI you can have a text conversation with.
  • 📘 Training data — The millions of books and articles AI learned from.

What AI Can Help With

  • ✅ Answering general questions
  • ✅ Drafting letters or emails
  • ✅ Explaining confusing documents
  • ✅ Getting recipe ideas
  • ✅ Planning a trip

Never Share with AI

  • ❌ Social Security number
  • ❌ Bank account or credit card numbers
  • ❌ Passwords or PINs
  • ❌ Your full Medicare or insurance ID
