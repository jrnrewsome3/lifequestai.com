What's Next: AI in Your Daily Life

You've come a long way. This final lesson looks ahead — and gives you practical tools to keep learning confidently on your own terms.

You're Already Ahead of Most People

The fact that you've worked through this curriculum puts you ahead of millions of people who simply feel confused or scared about AI. You now know what it is, how to use it, how to stay safe, and how to verify information.

AI is going to keep evolving — but the core skills you've built here will help you navigate whatever comes next. Let's look at a few more tools and ideas that are worth knowing about.

Tools Worth Knowing

📓 Obsidian — Your Personal Notebook

Obsidian is a free note-taking app that stores everything on your own computer — not in anyone's cloud. Think of it as a private digital notebook for keeping research, important documents, and notes from your AI conversations.

You can use it to store things like: the questions you want to ask your doctor, a summary of your insurance policy, or a list of AI responses you found helpful. Because it's stored locally, it's private and works even without internet.

💡

Download Obsidian free at obsidian.md. When you first open it, create a "vault" — just a folder on your computer — and start typing notes. It's simpler than it sounds.

📚 OpenStax — Free Textbooks for Curious Minds

OpenStax is a nonprofit that provides free, peer-reviewed textbooks on subjects like biology, history, psychology, economics, and more. If you've ever wanted to learn something deeply — not just Google it — OpenStax gives you the same quality material used in college courses, for free.

You can pair OpenStax with an AI: read a chapter, then ask Claude or ChatGPT to explain anything that confused you in plain language. It's like having a private tutor sitting next to you.

💡

Find free textbooks at openstax.org. All books are free to read online or download. No account required to browse.

🤖 AI Agents — What Are They?

You've been using AI like a conversation — you ask, it answers. But there's a newer type of AI called an agent that can take actions on your behalf, like a very capable assistant.

An AI agent might help you: search multiple websites and summarize what it finds, fill out a form, send an email on your behalf, or organize your calendar. Tools like Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot are already moving in this direction.

For now, the important thing to know: even when AI can take actions, you should always review what it's about to do before it does it. A good AI agent will ask "Are you sure?" before sending anything or making changes. That's a safety feature — not an annoyance.

⚠️

Agents and Safety

As AI becomes more capable of taking actions, safety becomes even more important. Only give an AI agent access to things you'd be comfortable with — and never give it access to your banking or financial accounts unless you fully understand and trust the tool.

🧩 How AI Keeps Getting Better

AI tools are improving rapidly. A few things to know:

  • They'll get better at understanding context — meaning you'll need to explain yourself less
  • They'll be built into more everyday devices — your phone, TV, hearing aids, and home appliances
  • New scam tactics will emerge — staying informed is the best defense
  • There will be regulation — governments around the world are working on rules for AI

The skills you've developed in this workshop — healthy skepticism, verifying information, protecting personal data — will remain valuable no matter how AI evolves.

The Mindset That Keeps You Safe

🌱

Stay curious, not fearful

AI is a tool. Like electricity or the telephone, it can be misused — but it can also make life better. Curiosity is your greatest asset.

🔍

Verify, then trust

Check important things. Not everything — that would be exhausting. But health, money, and safety decisions always deserve a second opinion.

🗣️

Talk about it

Share what you've learned with family and friends. Discussing AI openly — especially with grandchildren — helps everyone in your family stay safer.

📬

Keep learning at your own pace

You don't need to know everything. Focus on what's useful to you right now — health research, writing, or planning — and let your confidence grow from there.
